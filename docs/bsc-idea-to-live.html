Idea to live, in one record

What the design team described on 22 September, and how the PLM carries each step — with the same people, the same words, and Enigma still the master. Fifteen minutes to read; every screen named here exists in the prototype.

From the call with design · 22 Sep 2026, corrected 23 Sep against the MTM_Swatches and Swatch Books workbooksPrototype bsc-plm, walkthrough modeAuthor DevX Labs

Four things that stop existing

The swatch Slack channel
becomes seven dates on a row
The blue highlight
becomes a priority switch that sorts
The sticker made in Canva
prints off the record
Vendor updates re-typed across trackers
one status, on the record it is about

Five rules the whole flow follows

One record per thingA concept, a requirement, an option request. The conversation, the files and every date live on it — never beside it.
The next date is the only buttonA row shows what has happened and one thing to do. Whose move it is reads in the team's colour.
One vendor per optionSourcing puts one vendor against each option or style; design pushes the PO, rejects, or asks for more time. Rejected vendors stay as history.
Enigma stays the masterThe PLM records that a step was done, by whom, when — and holds only the two or three fields that change what happens next.
Go live is a decision, not a gateDesign switches a fabric on whenever it decides — before or after the swatch dates. The record keeps what was skipped. Online is its own date, about two weeks later.

02 · MTM intake, swatches, store activation

Cloth in → stores showing it

This is the MTM_Swatches tracker: Vendor · Fabric bulk received · Fabric team handover · Fabric code · Priority-1 · Sticker Link · Status, then per store a tracking number, a dispatch date and a receiving date. The warehouse does the inward and one person there cuts the swatches. The sticker column is empty today because the sticker is made elsewhere by hand.

TodayIn the PLM
Warehouse writes "Fabric Bulk Received date", then "Fabric Team handover date for swatches".Same two dates, same person, on the fabric's row on the Swatch desk — the next date is the only button. Warehouse owns the row until photos are shared.
"Priority-1" typed in a column; someone is then told what to cut first.Priority-1 is a switch on the row. Priority rows sort to the top; a priority not cut raises an alert to the warehouse.
Swatches cut 25 × 25, handed to shipping; photos posted in Slack so design knows they went; tracking numbers and receiving dates typed per store.Cut · Dispatched · Photos — dates on the row. "Dispatched" fans out one row per store underneath, and the store's own tap fills the receiving date. Design sees the row turn.
The "Sticker Link" column stays empty; a label sheet — fabric code, name – article, book, MRP, "Incl. of all taxes", three across — is made by hand in Word or Canva.Print sticker sheet on the record: the same five lines, three across, seven down, one label per design per store (five designs × 34 stores = 170 labels, 9 sheets). Printing writes the Sticker date. Change a name or an MRP and the sheet follows.
Stores are told to display via the Slack group; "Swatches to Stores: Live on …" typed in the fabric sheet.Stores told and Live in stores are design's two dates — and Go live is a button, whenever design decides, not the end of a sequence. Online is its own date (§03).

Where: Design → Swatch desk (warehouse and design share the row) · the same chips appear on the concept under 6 · Go live. Alerts: to the warehouse — priority not cut · cloth in 2+ days, not handed over; to design — dispatched, stores not told.

03 · MTM designs · product codes

One fabric, several designs — each with a product code

The fabric sheet says it plainly: Fabric code → Product Code → Design Name → Design Type → MRP, and one fabric appears against several designs. The product code is what the store and the website sell; the fabric code hangs off it. Sourcing creates the designs, while the swatches are being cut; marketing's final name replaces the working one later.

04 · Enigma data entry

Enigma stays the master

You were clear: the new system complements Enigma, it does not replace it. So the PLM does not carry the field list. It records that the configuration was done — by whom, when, against which template — and it holds exactly the attributes that change what happens next in the flow.

Stays in Enigma · flows to the website from there
  • Fabric colour, wash / no-wash, stretch, ply
  • Construction, pattern, thread count, GSM
  • Exact composition %
  • Website description, PDP filters
  • Product / category mapping
  • MTM customisation — contrast, piping applicability
Two flags the PLM does holdWhy
Large check / pattern (over about 2 in)Has to be matched in cutting, so fabric consumption goes up. If it is not flagged, the BOM and the order sheet under-count the cloth. The flag sits beside the consumption rule on the concept.
Composition family (e.g. "bamboo blend")The website filter, beside the exact composition. It decides where the product appears; design is the team that knows it.

Where: the concept → 6 · Go live → Configured in Enigma, and Enigma flags that drive the flow. Detailed field mapping is for the Bombay visit with your project manager and developer.

05 · Swatch books & MTM inventory planning

The assortment gap is what raises a request

The tracker is a picture of the physical swatch books — which fabric sits where, left and right — and you plan around roughly thirty active options. When some run low or come off, you count the gap and source replacements. That count is the real beginning of an option request, so the PLM starts it there rather than from a blank form.

TodayIn the PLM
Each fabric mapped to a named book; placement kept in the sheet.Swatch book on every fabric; the sticker and the swatch desk print it. Placement (left / right) is a field we add after seeing the books.
Weekly review; low stock marked by conditional formatting.Fabric master → Core · reorder points and Non-core · range gaps: cover in weeks against the mill's lead time, and Enigma's own auto-PO rule for core.
The range header carries the count — "Non-Core Structures (29)" — and the gap against ~30 is counted by hand; replacements sourced.MTM concepts shows every book against 30: FORMAL VOL.1 · 21/30 FORMAL VOL.2 · 22/30 → raise a request for 17 options — the request opens pre-filled with the count and the books that are short.
Fabric taken live → message to stores; tracker highlighted.Live in stores is a date on the concept and the desk; the row stops being "on the desk".

Where: MTM concepts (the strip above the list) · MTM fabric master · Option requests.

06 · RTW design → sourcing → sampling → production

RTW, brief to bulk, on one record

A requirement is one bulk style. It carries the numbers planning gives — Charanjeet's ratio and total pieces — the cloth brief, the BOM, and then everything that happens to it, in six numbered steps that read the same way on the list and in the drawer.

TodayIn the PLM
Styles and fabrics shortlisted; production emailed the list with costs; factory allocated.1 · Request — style, qty, ratio, target date and cost, the cloth brief, the BOM. Hand to sourcing is one click; the lead-time clock starts.
Sourcing collects quotes; design chooses by mail; someone raises a fabric PO.2 · Vendors — one vendor with terms. 3 · Purchase order — design pushes it; the fabric joins the master; sourcing chases it there. 4 · Fabric in — GRN at the unit.
Style sheet, BOM and job card typed per style. Factory makes a photo/PP sample; RQC comments; comments to the merchandiser.5 · Sampling — factory allocated on capacity; PP rounds with RQC's comments on the record. Style sheet · job card prints off the record — change the record and the sheet follows.
Bulk starts; status kept in several sheets; vendor updates collected and re-typed.RTW production tracker — one status per PO, TNA against the category's milestone template, escalations when a milestone slips. 6 · Go live — the launch, display and fit pieces, stores confirming, marketing brief.

Where: RTW requirements (design/planning) · Fabric sourcing (sourcing) · RTW production tracker (production) · RTW launches (planning). Inventory ready in ~45 days → swatches to stores → live; POS and online close together.

Three ways to buy the cloth

Discovery told us there are three, not two. The requirement asks which on day one, and the six steps change shape to match — who acts, and whether there is a PO to push at all.

Build order, and what to settle in Bombay

Everything above exists in the prototype today. The order below is what we would harden first for a pilot, by value against effort.

  1. Swatch desk with sticker generationthe flow you described in most detail; mostly dates; removes the channel, the highlight and the Canva file
    small
  2. Designs per fabric with product codes, and the label sheetthe master's own rows; one label per design per store
    small

  4. Assortment gap → option requestthe request starts from the number you already count

    medium
  6. Enigma link — configured on, by, the two flags; field mapping after the visitcomplement, never replace
    with PM & dev

Open questions for the visit

  • Product code format — the sheet has BOMSH02CM01310 and BOM-SHI-CU-497. Which is current, and what do the segments encode? We mint BOM-SHI-FM-nnnn until told.
  • Thirty per book, or thirty overall? We built it per swatch book; the prototype shows FORMAL VOL.1 at 21, LINEN at 67 — one of those numbers is wrong about how you count.
  • Swatch book placement — left / right, which page: a field, or a photo of the book?
  • Picario — swatches also go to Picario (digital swatch). One more "store" on the desk, or its own step?
  • Label stock — the sheet is 3 across; how many down, and which label paper? Line two today is the fabric name and the mill's article — should it become the design name and product code?
  • Enigma field ownership — which fields does design own outright, which does sourcing correct? This decides who gets edit rights.
  • RQC — the same people as production QC, or a separate desk that needs its own login?
  • FOB terms — is the label/trim charge always via BOM, and does BSC ever fund the cloth up front?
