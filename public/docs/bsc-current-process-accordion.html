BSC — Current Process (As-Is)

20 Oct 2026 · Riddhiman Ghosh · DevX Labs · Field lists are a first cut and will be corrected after further discovery.

How Bombay Shirt Company runs today, step by step: who does what, in which sheet or system, with which fields, and how long it takes. The optimized-process page mirrors these sections one for one.

Context
Purpose and how to read

This is the handover to the implementation team for Bombay Shirt Company's product lifecycle tool. Part A describes the process as BSC runs it today, step by step, so the team can check that what it builds aligns with how work actually happens. Part B specifies the target process: every record, screen, step, rule, alert and integration, in enough detail to build from without a further brief.

Audience. The engineers, the product owner and the QA lead building the tool; BSC's functional owners (design, sourcing, planning, production, warehouse, stores) for sign-off on Part A and on the open questions.

Where it comes from. Four discovery sittings, two days on site (27–31 Jul 2026), the sourcing call (6 Aug), the RTW inventory knowledge transfer (12 Aug), the store visit (13–14 Aug), a live read-only session in Enigma (26 Aug), the design discovery call (22 Sep), 22 working files shared by the teams, and the interactive process model reviewed with the sponsor through September. Nothing in Part A is invented; where a fact is unknown the step says so.

How to read.

  • Part A flows are numbered tables: step · who · trigger · data source · fields · format · time · hand-off. Format names the physical thing (a sheet tab, an Enigma screen, an email, a Slack channel, paper). Time is elapsed time as the team estimates it.

  • Part B flows are numbered tables: step · actor · screen and control · inputs · validations · writes · tells · target time. Section 13 consolidates the rules; Section 10 gives the state machines the rules protect.

  • Section 16 maps every current step to its target step so a reviewer can see what changes and what stays.

  • Field names in Part A are quoted as they appear in the sheet header. Field names in Part B are the target record's field names.

On data types, sources and fields. The columns, cadences and enumerations in Part A are what we saw in Aug–Sep 2026. The field lists in Part B are the first cut, mapped to those columns and to Enigma's live schema as read on 26 Aug. Both will be corrected on the Bombay visit and as Enigma API access widens; Section 20 lists what is still open. Build to the record shapes and the state machines; expect field names and enumerations to move.

Out of scope. Costing, margin, working capital, ageing and inventory valuation stay in their sheets. Customer marketing (Superfone), store checklists (OPSR) and any garment visualiser are not part of this build.

Glossary and status vocabularies

The words below are BSC's own; the tool uses them unchanged in lists, on records and in alerts. Every closed set in the second table is the literal TypeScript union or constant list in src/domain/types.ts / src/domain/reference.ts; the build must not paraphrase them.

TermMeaning
MTMMade to measure: the customer buys first, the shirt is made after (about 14 days; ~2 days for domestic fabric). Stock is fabric in metres, held in three buckets: CORE, NON_CORE, BESPOKE. The fabric is the SKU.
RTWReady to wear: made 3–4 months ahead to a forecast; stock is finished pieces by size. The garment (style-colour, per size) is the SKU.
FOBA garment bought finished from a vendor who buys the cloth and makes it. In the model this is sourcingMode = 'fob-nominated' (BSC names the supplier by email) or 'vendor-sources' (the vendor chooses its own cloth). 'bsc-buys' is the third mode: BSC buys the cloth and a CMT factory makes it.
RequirementAnything design asks for. On the Requirements screen a row is one of two kinds: MTM fabric (a Concept), RTW style (a ProductionOrder). The earlier third kind, several options at once (an OptionRequest, retired 20 Oct 2026; options are raised one by one).
ConceptThe record of one MTM fabric (kind: 'new-fabric') or construction (kind: 'construction'), from idea to live. Eleven derived gates; six design steps.
Design (on a fabric)One sellable shirt configuration on a fabric (ConceptDesign): product code, working name, final name, style, category Formal/Casual, MRP, cost, PDP copy. One fabric has several.
Product codeBSC's Shopify identifier for a design. The model mints BOM-SHI-FM-nnnn (Formal) or BOM-SHI-CS-nnnn (Casual) from a running sequence starting at 4200. Not the fabric code.
Fabric codeEnigma's identifier for a fabric (e.g. NOMSOXSOLI-1849), minted when design creates the datasheet. The model mints a stand-in (NO + 2 letters of the mill + 6 letters of the name + -16nn) only until Enigma's code is known.
Enigma codeOn an RTW order, enigmaCode is BSC's fabric code in Enigma for the bulk cloth (e.g. FORPDOSOPI-81).
Vendor fabric code / articleThe mill's own article number (vendorFabricCode on an order, vendorCode on a fabric or offer), e.g. Verdi SOK#24594. What goes on the PO.
Swatch bookA physical book of 25×25 cm fabric squares in every store; the MTM shopfront. Each MTM fabric belongs to one book: FORMAL SHIRTS - VOL.1, FORMAL SHIRTS - VOL.2, CASUAL SHIRTS VOL.1, LINEN SHIRTS; bespoke books: Leggiuno Bespoke. Store books come in three kinds (MTM, RTW, BESPOKE), edition AW26.
Block / holdA mill agrees by email, WhatsApp or call to hold cloth for a period with no money paid. Two shapes in the model: a Block record (metres, agreedOn, expiresOn from the vendor's blockWindowDays) and a per-offer holdUntil / blockValidUntil date on a quote.
ExtensionDesign (or planning) asks sourcing to get a hold extended before deciding; sourcing extends the date and sends the options back.
Option (request)One numbered slot on an option request ("Option 1 … Option N"). Sourcing puts ONE live vendor against a slot; design settles each slot on its own.
Offer / quoteA mill's terms against one ask: ₹/m, MOQ, width, lead days, hold-until, composition, note. FabricQuote on an RTW order; ConceptFabricOption on a concept or a request slot.
POPurchase order. Fabric PO to a mill in metres (FabricPo, numbered FPO-71nn; on an RTW order also FPO #24nn); production PO to a factory in pieces (ProductionOrder.poNo, PO #155nn). Raising is not receiving.
Auto-POEnigma's threshold top-up: when a core fabric's on-hand plus incoming falls to the reorder point, a PO for the MOQ (or the multiples needed) goes to the mill. Domestic core: Enigma fires it (source: 'auto'); international: a person raises it (source: 'manual').
Reorder point (ROP)max(lead-time demand, vendor MOQ), where lead-time demand = last-8-week shirts/week × 2.15 m × lead days ÷ 7. The suggested PO is max(MOQ, ceil(shortfall ÷ MOQ) × MOQ).
SaleablestockMetres − reservedMetres − thresholdMetres; Enigma's own formula (physical − accounting − threshold), recomputed by the tool. Store rule of thumb: under 15 m is "Low Stock".
GRNGoods received note. Every inward in the model raises one (GRN-26-nnnn), of one of six kinds: fabric, rtw-po, bespoke-parcel, mtm-round, trims, store-inward.
STOStock transfer order from the hub to one store, sent in waves ("STO Series No."), with a type and a hemming type.
Vehicle roundThe daily vehicle that brings finished MTM pieces from the factories to the unit; each piece is received (RTS scan), short (not on the vehicle) or rejected (turned back with a reason).
RTS"Return to source": the unit's scan that a finished piece is back at the production unit and can be shipped.
PP samplePre-production sample; decision: 'approved' is the PP go-ahead that lets bulk start. resubmit means major comments.
Shipment sampleA concept's second gate after PP: the sample made at scale.
Green slipThe production unit's comment sheet on a sample round (comments[] with area and major flag).
DropA month's RTW launch (Launch.dropMonth, e.g. Oct 26); one launch record per option with six gates.
Display & fit piecesPieces issued to every RTW-holding store at COGS, never sellable: one display piece (shirts size 40, bottoms 32) and a fit set (shirts 38/40/42, bottoms 30/32/34).
Fit sample set (MTM)The three fits (Slim, Standard, Heavy) × four sizes (38, 40, 42, 44) each store holds so the customer can try the fit before the shirt is made.
BillOne customer visit with one or more pieces (billNo, BILL-xxxxx); each piece is its own CustomerOrder.
Order sheetEnigma's MTM sheet: 22 option tiles (SheetOptionKey), 20 measurements (MeasureKey), 4 pattern flags. Every edit after punching is a numbered revision.
PP (MTM stage)"Lying with us": punched, freeze window over, waiting for a factory plan.
Freeze windowOrders punched in the last 2 hours (PP_FREEZE_HOURS, adjustable 0–24) are not planned because stores still change them.
PlanAn MTM production plan (#413nn): one factory, one category, a set of order sheets sent with the fabric. Capacity is a cap; forcing over it is recorded with a name.
HeadroomWhat a factory can take today: perDay − in transit − max(0, WIP − 2×perDay), where perDay is the factory's dailyTarget or its 7-day average out.
AgingAn MTM piece in-factory for more than 5 days.
Extra fabricThe daily "EXTRA FABRIC" email as a record: metres, one of five reasons, one of six panels, against one piece of a bundle. Approval deducts the metres.
CaseA return or alteration raised on one delivered piece (RET-41nn), with a resolution ladder and, for MTM, a factory leg or a hemming-unit leg.
LadderThe order of rungs a store tries: alteration → refit → remake → store-credit → refund; exchange (swap from the shelf, RTW) is a side door.
Hemming unitThe unit that hems bottoms and alters pieces, reached through the warehouse; seven scanned hops there and back.
Store creditA CreditEntry (+ given on a case, − spent on a bill); balance is the sum per customer name.
Write-offA store removes a damaged or unsellable piece from system stock the moment it decides (reason, disposition).
Option (RTW)One style in one colour; sizes are not options. styleOfOption strips the colour after " - ".
Ideal option countPlanning's per-sub-category target (e.g. Casual Solid 35 = 10 core + 25 non-core). A gap against it is what raises an option request. MTM plans around ~30 active options (MTM_ACTIVE_TARGET).
ROS / AWSRate of sale: the model's "true ROS" is the mean of the last 4 in-stock weeks; "top-6 ROS" is the mean of the best 6 in-stock weeks since launch; "naive" is the 12-week mean. The sheets call this AWS.
WOFWeeks of forward cover = stock ÷ true ROS.
Velocity gradeA ≥10/wk · B ≥5 · C ≥2 · D ≥0.1 · E not selling.
OTIFOn time (Dispatch actual ≤ target in-house) and in full (delivered within ±3% of cut or order qty).
TNATime and action: the per-PO milestone calendar generated from the category template (22 possible milestones, day offsets from PO date).
BufferDays between expected in-house and target in-house; ≤ 7 is critical, ≤ 10 at risk.
Mention@person or @role in a comment; addressed to that side on that record until resolved.
Walkthrough modeThe prototype's super-admin switch: every screen and button open whoever you are signed in as; each still says whose step it is.
Quiet modeGuidance off: no explainers, no example strips, full lists.

1. MTM fabric, idea to live

Design's job in one line (design team walkthrough, 7 Oct 2026). Design starts with a requirement, helps sourcing close the right fabric, turns that one fabric into one or more sellable designs, supplies the product information, and stays with it until it can be made and launched. Three things are never the same object: the requirement is not the fabric (design states the need, sourcing closes an option); the fabric is not the design (one fabric code carries several designs, each with its own preset, design code, cost, MRP and description, written on the design sheet); and a design that exists is not a design that is live (swatches reach stores, garments are inwarded, and online shoots finish on their own dates). A design carries a temporary name from design until marketing gives the final name and description.

An MTM fabric is "just fabric": the vendor lead time is 2 days for a Bombay mill and about 2 months from China (Sairam, 30 Jul 2026), but the path from the design meeting to a store shelf still crosses 20–30 days of design, tech-pack and costing work before a PO can go out (Sreekanth, 6 Aug), a block that lives in three copies of a sheet with no expiry date, an Enigma datasheet, the fabric ordering sheet, the swatch tracker, the weekly "bible" and the REVISED NAME tab — the same fabric typed 3 to 5 times by different people (Discovery Record v1.2 §01). No single record follows it; the end-to-end duration is not measured anywhere (to confirm — the "8 to 14 weeks" in earlier drafts is an estimate, not a number BSC gave).

12. Bespoke, mill to customer

Bespoke is the third made-to-measure bucket: about 1,500 premium options across 5+ mills that BSC does not stock. The mill holds the cloth and cuts a length for each customer order, so whether a fabric can be sold depends entirely on what the mill reported that day. It is about 26% of MTM units (827 of 3,196) and 30–40% of MTM revenue (the two discovery notes disagree on the split).
