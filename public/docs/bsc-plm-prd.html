1. Purpose and scope

BSC PLM is one working system for the ten processes Bombay Shirt Company runs today across Enigma, Shopify and about twenty-two spreadsheets, so that a fabric, a style or a customer's shirt is typed once and every team reads the same record. It is built beside Enigma and connected over Enigma's API: Enigma stays the system of record for fabrics, customer orders, purchase orders and stock; BSC PLM reads what it needs and keeps its own records for everything Enigma does not hold (plans, trackers, cases, GRNs, swatch desk, planning reviews). Write-back to Enigma (POs, on-hold flags, GRNs, STOs) comes in the last phase, where access is agreed.

This PRD is organised by workflow, in the order the teams go live: the store, MTM production, the factory merchandiser, the warehouse, design, RTW production, then planning and the rest. Each workflow states who starts it, the user flow step by step, what the screen shows and asks, where each piece of data comes from (Enigma, Shopify, a sheet, a team member, or computed by BSC PLM), what the system checks, what happens next and who is told. The prototype at the working copy is the reference for every screen named here; the implementation handover (sections A and B) holds the as-is detail, the data model and the state machines this PRD summarises.

In scope: MTM fabric lifecycle, swatch book requests, RTW style from request to launch, RTW planning, MTM daily production, the factory merchandiser portal, the store, the warehouse, replenishment and stock health, bespoke, and the cross-cutting alerts, files and conversation.

Out of scope for phase 1 to 6: forecasting models (tested later against the planning record), costing and margin, working capital, customer marketing, store checklists, the MTM visualiser, and escalations as a workflow. FOB garments are retired: BSC buys the cloth for every bulk style.

Conventions. Dates are the record's own. Quantities are pieces unless the unit says metres. "Design" includes planning wherever planning works on the same list. Every rule marked later is specified but not built in the prototype.

4. Workflow 1 — Store

The store sells one piece at a time and resolves problems one piece at a time. A visit is one bill with several pieces, each its own order with its own size, inseam and fit. The store manager's first question on a complaint is "can I fix it here?". The store page has five groups: customers, orders, what I can sell, stock in and out, returns and alterations. Anyone not from that store can read it but not act.

The production unit runs its day from one screen: Allocate, Plans, Factory floor, Changed sheets, Extra fabric. The system proposes the plan from each factory's spare capacity and a person confirms it. Every move of a piece is a scan. 15. Cross-cutting behaviour

The bell. One bell per person showing only their team's items; every line opens the record at the step. The list is recomputed from the records on every change; nothing is stored or emailed, and a line disappears the moment the record moves. Lines sort by the cost of ignoring them, not by age. Levels: critical, warn, info. The owner sees every role. Store and factory users see only their scope. The full rule list is in the handover's alert engine section; every workflow above lists its own.

Files and conversation on every record. Each record (requirement, option, PO, fabric, case, launch, customer, vendor, planning review) carries attachments with a label per kind (sketch, tech pack, job card, trim card, BOM, swatch photo, planning note, sheet) and a conversation with mentions by role or person. Mentions put a line on the mentioned person's bell. Nothing is emailed; the record is the thread.

Who acts next. Every record header carries one state pill. A button appears only for the role that owns the step; others see whose turn it is. The plain view hides explainers and example strips; a demo setting turns them on.

Lists. Paged at 20 to 25; filters and the open record live in the URL so a view is shareable and Back works; every list exports to .xlsx; saved views in the sidebar per person. Search across POs, orders, fabrics, options, mills, factories, requests, cases, launches, customers and stores.

Settings. Tracker settings (days per milestone per category, risk thresholds, in-full tolerance) replace the workbook's Config and Auto Plan Logic tabs and re-plan open POs on save. MTM: freeze window, factory capacity inputs. Replenishment constants (metres per shirt, growth uplift for RTW, cover targets, MOQ default) are placed in one reference and flagged to confirm.

Undo and audit. The last ten writes can be undone for seven seconds, only by the person who made them. Every write on a record appends who, when and what; order sheets keep numbered revisions with from and to; planning reviews keep every assumption; ship-by moves keep the original date and the reason.

Print and export. Sticker page, outbound cut sheet, the factory's day; later the job card, style sheet, BOM, GRN and round-trip slip on A4.
