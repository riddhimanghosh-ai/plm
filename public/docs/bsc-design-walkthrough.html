BSC PLM · for the design team

Design, end to end

Every process the design team runs in BSC PLM, from the first ask to the day it goes live.

8 Oct 2026 · Riddhiman Ghosh · DevX Labs

Design works from one list, Requirements. Every ask starts with + New requirement, which asks one question: an MTM fabric, several fabric options at once, or an RTW style.

Each ask moves through the same five stages, and the list shows which one it is at: Requirement, Sourcing, Finalised and ordered, Downstream, then Live.

The three flows

1

Made to measure

An MTM fabric, from idea to live

One new fabric, shade or construction variant for the swatch books.

  1. Raise the askDesign

    Pick the kind: a new fabric or shade, or a construction variant (collar, cuff or placket) on a fabric already in stock. Give a working name and why, and optionally the swatch book and the month it is wanted.

  2. Sample and approve, if neededProduction unit · Design

    The pre-production sample and the approval to every department are optional. Skip them with a reason, for example for a new shade of a known fabric.

  3. One vendor against the askSourcing

    Mill, article number, price per metre, minimum order, lead time and how long the mill will hold the cloth. Then send it to design.

  4. DecideDesign

    Push the fabric PO, reject the vendor with a reason, or ask for more time. A lapsed hold has to be extended by sourcing first.

  5. PO raisedSystem

    The PO mints the Enigma fabric code and the cloth lands on the swatch desk.

  6. Swatch deskWarehouse · Stores

    Received, handed to swatches, cut, sticker printed, dispatched, photographed. Each store confirms it arrived.

  7. Set up for saleSourcing · Design

    Sourcing adds the designs on the fabric. Design sets it up in Enigma and adds marketing’s name and the price.

  8. Go liveDesign

    Tell stores, write the story, go live in stores. Online follows about two weeks later, once photos, video, post-production and the product page are done.

2

Made to measure

Several options at once

Used to fill a swatch book, for example “ten winter shirts”.

  1. See the gapDesign

    The swatch book tiles show active options against a plan of 30 per book.

  2. Raise the requestDesign

    Category, count, live-by date, target cost and the bill of materials.

  3. Write each option’s askDesign

    Look, composition, colour, weight, a reference fabric, the top price per metre and notes, per option.

  4. Fill the optionsSourcing

    One vendor against each option, then send it to design.

  5. Settle each optionDesign

    Each option is one line with a Review button. Push the PO, reject, or ask for more time. Every option has its own hold.

  6. Close what will not happenDesign · Sourcing

    Either side closes an option with a reason; design can reopen it. The request completes when every option is ordered or closed.

  7. Ordered options carry on as MTM fabricsSystem

    Each starts at “PO raised”, already approved, and runs the swatch desk and go-live steps above.

3

Ready to wear

An RTW style, from request to launch

One record per style in four sections: the request, factory finalisation, fabric finalisation, then milestones and final steps. Design owns the first and the two POs.

  1. Proto firstProduction unit · Design

    The unit makes the style on any shirt fabric to hand. Design approves it, sends it back, or skips it with a reason for a repeat.

  2. Send the requestDesign

    • Style, category, fit and order quantity
    • Size ratio across 28–50 or XS–4XL
    • Target price and target in-house date
    • The cloth wanted and the trims
    • Job card, trim card, BOM and tech pack

    Missing documents are flagged as owed but do not block. The style shows on the RTW tracker as “no PO yet”.

  3. Factory decisionProduction (Nitin) · Design

    Nitin proposes a factory with a cost and an in-house date. Design accepts, or rejects with a comment and he proposes again.

  4. Factory PODesign

    Design raises it and the documents travel with it. The milestone plan is drawn from the PO date.

  5. Fabric POSourcing · Design

    From the day the request lands, sourcing puts one vendor against the cloth. Design pushes the fabric PO exactly as for MTM. The PO mints the Enigma code and fills the tracker’s fabric details by itself.

  6. Short clothMerchandiser · Design

    If the factory counts less usable cloth than needed, the merchandiser or production asks. Design gives a modified size ratio or a cut short.

  7. Design steps outProduction · Warehouse · Planning

    Nitin and the factory merchandiser run milestones, QC and dispatch. The warehouse receives the lots and planning puts the style into a drop.

Design’s standing jobs

Pausing and reactivating fabrics. Sourcing checks core stock weekly and escalates a fabric whose supply is delayed or out. Design pauses it, which hides it from stores and the order form, and reactivates it when stock is back.

Promoting non-core to core. Design decides; sourcing then agrees a supply block with the mill.

Bespoke. No design step. The cloth stays at the mill and is ordered per customer order, never as a requirement.

Open item
