Context
Purpose and how to read

This is the handover to the implementation team for Bombay Shirt Company's product lifecycle tool. Part A describes the process as BSC runs it today, step by step, so the team can check that what it builds aligns with how work actually happens. Part B specifies the target process: every record, screen, step, rule, alert and integration, in enough detail to build from without a further brief.

Audience. The engineers, the product owner and the QA lead building the tool; BSC's functional owners (design, sourcing, planning, production, warehouse, stores) for sign-off on Part A and on the open questions.

Where it comes from. Four discovery sittings, two days on site (27–31 Jul 2026), the sourcing call (6 Aug), the RTW inventory knowledge transfer (12 Aug), the store visit (13–14 Aug), a live read-only session in Enigma (26 Aug), the design discovery call (22 Sep), 22 working files shared by the teams, and the interactive process model reviewed with the sponsor through September. Nothing in Part A is invented; where a fact is unknown the step says so.

How to read.

  • Part A flows are numbered tables: step · who · trigger · data source · fields · format · time · hand-off. Format names the physical thing (a sheet tab, an Enigma screen, an email, a Slack channel, paper). Time is elapsed time as the team estimates it.

  • Part B flows are numbered tables: step · actor · screen and control · inputs · validations · writes · tells · target time. Section 13 consolidates the rules; Section 10 gives the state machines the rules protect.

  • Section 16 maps every current step to its target step so a reviewer can see what changes and what stays.

  • Field names in Part A are quoted as they appear in the sheet header. Field names in Part B are the target record's field names.

On data types, sources and fields. The columns, cadences and enumerations in Part A are what we saw in Aug–Sep 2026. The field lists in Part B are the first cut, mapped to those columns and to Enigma's live schema as read on 26 Aug. Both will be corrected on the Bombay visit and as Enigma API access widens; Section 20 lists what is still open. Build to the record shapes and the state machines; expect field names and enumerations to move.

Out of scope. Costing, margin, working capital, ageing and inventory valuation stay in their sheets. Customer marketing (Superfone), store checklists (OPSR) and any garment visualiser are not part of this build.

LadderThe order of rungs a store tries: alteration → refit → remake → store-credit → refund; exchange (swap from the shelf, RTW) is a side door.
Hemming unitThe unit that hems bottoms and alters pieces, reached through the warehouse; seven scanned hops there and back.
Store creditA CreditEntry (+ given on a case, − spent on a bill); balance is the sum per customer name.
Write-offA store removes a damaged or unsellable piece from system stock the moment it decides (reason, disposition).
Option (RTW)One style in one colour; sizes are not options. styleOfOption strips the colour after " - ".

The spine and the principles

Everything design asks for is a requirement, and every requirement moves through the same four stages, whatever kind of thing it is. Stage 3 (finalised and ordered) is what sets everything downstream moving. After stage 3, design decides only three things: the name, the price, and when the piece goes live.

flowchart LR
  A[1 Requirement<br/>design writes the ask] --> B[2 Sourcing finds and blocks<br/>one vendor per ask]
  B --> C[3 Finalised and ordered<br/>PO raised]
  C --> D[4 Downstream<br/>warehouse, production, planning, stores do the work]
  D --> E[Live<br/>stores first, then online]

Principles every flow follows.

  1. One record per thing, and one link opens it from anywhere. That covers a concept, a style, an options request, a customer's piece, a return case, a fabric and a launch. A link that was shared once keeps working even if the page it sits on changes.

  2. Every change of state is written on the record as a dated entry that says who made it. Nobody retypes a status. Where a thing stands, whether it is healthy and whether it is at risk are all worked out from those entries. Nobody edits them by hand.

  3. One team owns each step. The record names that team and tells it through one notification bell. Owner labels always name the team, never "yours".

  4. Enigma stays the master for fabric and design attributes and for the stock ledger. The tool notes that Enigma was set up, by whom and when. It never recreates what Enigma holds.

  5. The store sells by piece. A bill can hold several pieces, and each piece becomes its own order for the factory.

  6. The tool uses the same word for a state that people use (Section 1). Lists show only those words.

  7. Every record is laid out the same way:

    • the headline (where it stands and whose move it is)

    • the step that is open now

    • the other steps, folded away

    • the document that prints from the record

    • files, conversation and activity, when there are any

  8. Buttons say what they do. Forms ask only for what the step needs and keep the rest behind "more". Help text is not part of the product.

  9. Finance is out of scope. Costing, margins and working capital stay in finance's sheets.

  10. Email and Slack are never the record of truth. When a message goes out, it goes from the record and is noted on it.

Teams, screens and permissions

Nine roles, eighteen navigation entries under seven groups, one bell. A person sees only the screens their team works in (NAV[].roles), and a few entries are hidden from a role that could technically reach them (hideFor) so each team lands on its own list. The Owner sees everything; "Walkthrough mode" (demoAll) makes any login act as owner for the prototype, while every step still says whose it is (ScreenOwner strip: "{Team} work this screen."). A screen a role does not work in is not reachable even by URL — it redirects to the role's home (homeFor = the first NAV entry that lists the role; the fallback is /design).

Data model

Twenty-six collections on one Db object (src/domain/types.ts), persisted as one document (zustand persist, key bsc-plm-v1, version 96). Every field below is listed with its TypeScript type, whether it is optional (? = optional key; | null = nullable), and where it is born: E read from Enigma today or clearly Enigma's, S seeded from a sheet at go-live, T typed by a person in the tool, C computed by the tool. Dates are ISO strings; money is rupees as number; metres are number with two decimals (round2). Only ProductionOrder carries an activity[] log; other records carry their history in typed sub-objects (revisions, scans, attempts, nameHistory) or on the thread. Prototype identifiers are minted from Date.now().toString(36); the human numbers are sequential (REQ-nnn, FPO-71nn, PO #155nn, BSC-731nn, RET-41nn, GRN-26-nnnn, #413nn).

State machines

Each record's stage is derived from these transitions; the actions in store.ts are the only ways to move, and the guards in B13 decide whether a move is written. A transition not listed is not offered.

Sourcing on an RTW/FOB style (ProductionOrder.status × sourcing.stage).

  • Requirement → Quoting (vendors): handToSourcing (design/planning) or the first addFabricQuote.

  • vendors → design-choice: submitToDesign (needs ≥ 1 quote). A second addFabricQuote replaces the unchosen quote ("Vendor replaced · … withdrawn by sourcing").

  • design-choice → extension: askExtension (design or planning; the team that clicked is logged).

  • extension → design-choice: extendQuote then sendBackToDesign (marks the extension resolved).

  • design-choice | extension → vendors: rejectFabricQuote or removeFabricQuote on the last quote (stage, submittedOn and extension reset).

  • design-choice → chosen and status Allocated: chooseFabricVendor (sets fabricVendorId, fabricMetres, costing.cpm).

  • chosen → design-choice and status Quoting: unchooseFabricVendor (only while no fabric PO).

  • chosen → po-raised: raiseOrderFabricPo; or design-choice → po-raised and Allocated in one click: acceptQuoteAndRaisePo (Push PO). Both mint the fabric row on the master, a FabricPo, and land the "Fabric PO" milestone.

  • Allocated → Open: allocateFactory (planning; refused while a nominated fabric has no PO) — mints poNo, poDate, merchant, TNA, materials.

  • Open → Closed: receiveLot when the balance reaches zero or closeShort; legacy receive.

  • Cancelled: no action sets it (seed only).

  • FOB: setSourcingMode flips fabricNominated; markFobEmailed stamps the nomination. The "Vendors" step is done when the nomination is emailed; no PO step.

Sourcing on a concept (Concept.fabricOptions, optionsSentOn, fabricId, blockId, fabricPo).

  • add vendor (conceptAddFabricOption, replaces the live one; refused after a PO) → conceptSendOptionsToDesign sets optionsSentOn.

  • design: conceptAcceptAndRaisePo (Push PO — selects, mints the fabric on the master if needed, raises the FabricPo, sets fabricPo); conceptRejectFabricOption (last live one clears optionsSentOn → back to sourcing); conceptAskOptionExtension → sourcing conceptExtendOption → conceptSendOptionsBack; sourcing conceptRemoveFabricOption (withdraw).

  • older path still in the store, no button: conceptChooseFabric, conceptChooseFabricOption (choose without PO) → conceptBlockFabric (Vendor has blocked it, creates a Block held for the vendor's window) → conceptRaiseFabricPo (converts the block); conceptUnchooseFabric only while no block and no PO.

  • concept lifecycle: conceptSendPat → conceptSubmitSample('pp') → conceptReviewSample (resubmit = another round; approved) → conceptSubmitSample('shipment') → review → conceptSendApproval (posts the @planning @production @warehouse @store comment) → priceFabric → Enigma (conceptConfigureEnigma, conceptSetEnigmaFlags) → designs (conceptAddDesign / update / flags / remove) → conceptSetName → conceptSetMarketing → conceptGoLive (sets liveOn, clears the fabric's onHold) → online steps. conceptGoLive is not gated on the swatch desk; "Live" on the Requirements list additionally needs online.liveOn.

Option slot (RequestOption.status).

  • open → (sourcing addRequestOffer, replaces the live vendor) → with-design via sendOptionToDesign.

  • with-design → po-raised: acceptOfferAndRaisePo (mints the fabric on the master with onHold: true, the FabricPo, and a new-fabric Concept with approvalSentOn set so the swatch desk picks it up).

  • with-design → extension: askOptionExtension; extension → with-design: extendRequestOffer + sendOptionBack.

  • with-design | extension → open: rejectRequestOffer on the last live offer, or removeRequestOffer (sourcing withdraws).

  • any except po-raised → closed: closeOption (reason); closed → with-design or open: reopenOption.

  • Request stage on the list: 3 once every live slot has its PO; 2 while any slot is open / with design / extension / done; 1 otherwise.

Requirement stage (computed on the Requirements list). RTW style: 1 while status = Requirement; 2 until the fabric PO exists (or, for FOB / vendor-sources, until the nomination / no PO needed); 3 once poDone; 4 once production has begun (fabric ready, a material received, a factory allocated, or a sample); 5 (Live) once its launch is launched. Return case (stage plus the last attempt's outcome).

  • raised (raiseReturn, slaDays 10) → startResolution(step, where): alteration at store (nothing travels) · alteration at factory or remake (MTM: factoryLeg opened) · alteration at hemming-unit (hemLeg opened, destination store) · refit · exchange (RTW: accepted and closed on the spot; stock moves) · store-credit (accepted, refunded, closed, ledger entry) · refund (refund requested, stage back-at-store, waits on finance).

  • factory leg: sendReturnToFactory (at-factory) → factoryReceivedReturn → returnDoneAtFactory (back-at-store) → store counts it in (storeInward('return') sets backAtStoreOn).

  • hemming leg: sendReturnToHemming (at-factory) → hemLegStep inwardAtWarehouse → atUnit → hemmed → backAtWarehouse → dispatched (back-at-store) → delivered.

  • decideResolution: accepted → closed; not enough → the next rung opens. moveRefund (finance; no button): refunded, closed. advanceReturn (raised → at-factory → back-at-store → refunded → closed) exists with no button.

Launch gates (launchOf). In production (no receivedAtHo) → At HO → Allocating (allocateLaunch) → Dispatching (dispatchLaunch) → Ready when all six gates hold (received at HO, allocated, all dispatched, all received, marketing brief, launch date) → Launched (updateLaunch({launchedOn})). Nothing in the model blocks launchedOn on the gates (blocking on name and dispatch is to build).

RTW option. active ⇄ inactive (decideOption('make-inactive' | 'liquidate') with reason; 'reintroduce' clears it and optionally adds incomingQty); 'promote-to-core' sets core; 'reorder' adds incomingQty (default 150). The verb is recomputed on every read, not nightly.

Swatch desk. Each of the seven dates is set once by its button, in any order (the screen offers the next unset one); dispatched also creates one SwatchDispatch per store, which the store confirms. Swatch books / fit samples. dispatched → received (store or storeInward); fit sample state ok / worn / missing by the store.

Vehicle round (receiveRound). per piece: received → RTS scan + one mtm-round GRN per factory; short → receiving.shortOn; rejected → back to in-factory with the reason appended to remarks.

Stock request. requested → accepted | declined | shipped → received (the asking store's storeInward('request') also sets received).

Implementation plan — phased by team, built on Enigma

This is the order to build in, not the order the process runs in. Enigma stays the system of record throughout — every phase below reads from it, and only moves to writing into it where BSC and the Enigma team have agreed access. The plan is deliberately high-level: which team gets a working screen, roughly what it lets them do, and why that team goes when it does. The exact fields, click paths and edge cases are for the technical discovery that follows this document, working from the detail already laid out flow by flow above.

Before phase 1: a short foundations step — connect read-only to Enigma, set up one login per team and per store or factory, and build the shared page shell (the record view, the notification bell, search, printing) that every later phase reuses. Nothing user-facing ships from this step on its own.

Phase 1 — MTM production and the factory merchandiser, built together. This is the first team-facing release, because it is where the floor's day is most painfully manual today: a planner working from memory and a WhatsApp thread per factory, and no way for a factory to ask for more cloth without a phone call. Bespoke orders ride the same plan: a customer's bespoke shirt waits outside the plan until its cut length has arrived from the mill, then runs like any other made-to-measure order.

  • For the production planner: the daily allocation screen. It proposes which factory each punched order should go to, based on each factory's spare capacity for the day, and lets the planner accept the proposal, override it, or send a batch to a chosen factory. This is the piece the planner uses every morning.

  • For the factory merchandiser, at the same time: their own screen to see what has been sent to their factory, to log the day's output, and to ask for extra cloth when a piece is spoiled — replacing the daily WhatsApp and email.

  • On the production side, alongside the merchandiser's screen: approving an extra-cloth request, which deducts the metres from stock automatically the moment it is approved, instead of someone adjusting Enigma by hand later.

  • Also in this phase: the vehicle round (what came back from each factory today, matched against what was declared) and the running view of how many days a piece has sat in a factory.

Done when a planner and every MTM factory's merchandiser are working from these screens for real orders, and the WhatsApp thread for allocation and extra cloth is no longer the record of what happened.

Phase 2 — Store manager. Bills with more than one piece, a case raised on a delivered piece with the six-rung ladder (alter it here, swap from the shelf, send it to the factory or the hemming unit, refit, remake, or give the money back), store credit, and the store's own view of parcels coming in, stock asks to other stores, and damaged stock write-offs. A bespoke fabric can be picked only while the mill's last report says it is available. This is store-facing and customer-facing, so it goes after the MTM floor has proven the pattern.

Done when a handful of pilot stores are running returns and alterations through this instead of Slack, and store credit has one ledger instead of living in someone's memory.

Phase 3 — Warehouse. Every receipt — fabric rolls, ready-to-wear bulk, bespoke parcels, the vehicle round from MTM factories — writes one numbered goods-received note, always against the purchase order or parcel it belongs to. This phase also covers stock transfers to stores, swatch books and fit samples, and the round trip through the hemming unit. Bespoke lives here too: the mill's daily list typed or pasted in (a fabric reported out goes on hold everywhere at once), the call to the mill with BSC's parcel codes, and the parcel inwarded against its order. It goes after the store phase because by then both sides of most warehouse hand-offs (the factory feeding it, the store receiving from it) are already live.

Done when a fabric receipt updates saleable stock everywhere within the day, and a stock transfer that has sat unclosed for a week shows up as a problem on the warehouse's own list, not as ghost stock nobody notices.

Phase 4 — Design and sourcing. One list for every kind of ask — a new MTM fabric, several fabric options at once, a ready-to-wear style — moving through the same stages: raised, one vendor found and its terms agreed, the purchase order pushed, then downstream. A ready-to-wear style carries its bill of materials and tech pack before it goes to sourcing, with the fabric line as one cloth or a shortlist sourcing picks from; a made-to-measure fabric's sample and department approval are optional, skipped with a reason. This also covers the swatch desk (the six steps from cloth arriving to stores being told) and settling several options from one request. It comes after the three phases above because design and sourcing can keep working over email and Enigma directly in the meantime, whereas the floor teams in phases 1–3 have no such fallback.

Done when ten fabrics run from raised to stores told without the swatch tracker sheet or a hand-made sticker.

Phase 5 — Replenishment and the sales review. The reorder rule for MTM core fabrics (so nobody has to remember to check a mill), the nine-word action list for every ready-to-wear style (reorder it, pull it back, leave it, and so on), and the weekly sales review, daily sales report and stylist scorecard read from one page instead of rebuilt by hand each week. Bespoke has no reorder point — the mill's list is its only availability — so this phase only reads it (the bespoke tab of the weekly review).

Done when the international-fabric spreadsheet and the weekly stock-health scan are retired.

Phase 6 — RTW production. The equivalent for ready-to-wear: one record per style in four sections, as Nitin's team runs it. The request from design. Factory finalisation, where Nitin proposes a factory, cost and date, and design accepts or rejects with a comment and raises the factory PO with the job card, trim card, BOM and tech pack. Fabric finalisation, which is sourcing's one-vendor loop: the fabric PO mints the Enigma code and fills the tracker's fabric details, the cloth goes to the factory, and a short delivery gets a modified ratio or a cut short. Then the milestones and final steps, kept by Nitin and the factory merchandiser from the merchandiser's own portal. A style shows on the tracker from the request, marked "no PO yet". This covers the prototype on a stand-in fabric before any cloth is sourced, the sample rounds, the other BOM items checked against stock and signed off by sourcing after the fabric PO, materials issued to the factory, the day-by-day milestones (cutting, stitching, finishing, dispatch), quality checks before a lot ships, and receiving goods in lots against the order. It comes after design, sourcing and replenishment because half of every tracker row is written by planning, design and sourcing before production touches it, and every row is born from a requirement and carries its fields through; planning and category set the requirement, design the style and fabric specification, sourcing the vendor and the fabric PO, Nitin the factory proposal, the factory merchandiser the receipt of cloth and trims, the production unit and station merchandiser the revised and actual dates and escalations, and the warehouse's receipt closes the row. Production and the warehouse work from this together. It is the tracker sheet as the team reads it today — PO, style, vendor, merchant, quantity, where it is, its date calendar — with every step below hanging off a row on it, and each row born from design's requirement. The workbook's Config and Auto Plan Logic tabs become the tracker's settings (days from PO per milestone per category, risk thresholds, in-full tolerance; saving re-plots open POs' planned dates), and its other four tabs — OTIF dashboard, daily monitor, escalation log, merchandiser alerts — become one analytics screen for the RTW planner, read off the tracker.

Done when one month's cohort of ready-to-wear styles is tracked end to end on the tracker, and the old production spreadsheet is retired for that cohort.

Phase 7 — Writing into Enigma, and the remaining edges. Wherever Enigma access has been agreed by then: purchase orders, fabric on-hold flags, goods-received notes and stock transfers get written back into Enigma instead of only being read from and recorded alongside it. This phase also picks up whatever was deliberately left for later in the phases above — smaller screens sized for a phone, bulk actions, and the handful of rules and alerts flagged "later" throughout this document.

Running two systems side by side. For every phase, the spreadsheet or habit it replaces stays available, read-only, for one month after go-live. Each week, someone compares the new tool against the old sheet with the team that owns it, before that sheet is finally switched off.

Open questions and assumptions

Assumptions the build stands on until the Bombay follow-ups and the Enigma API review say otherwise. Each changes a field, a source or an owner above. Constants in the model that are placed rather than sourced are flagged in reference.ts comments and repeated here.

Assumed, to confirm.

  • Product-code format and who mints it (BOM-SHI-FM/CS-nnnn, sequence from 4200, minted when a design is added; the model lets design, sourcing and the warehouse add designs).

  • The MTM assortment target of about 30 active options (MTM_ACTIVE_TARGET), the same for every book; the per-family RANGES targets (Poplin 20, Twill 18, Oxford 12, Dobby 8, Linen 14, Stripes 30, Checks 24, Print 10) are placed, not from a sheet.

  • A date or id range on the ledger — decides whether ROS and consumption can be read at all without 8,585 calls.

  • Whether Shopify sales can be read per product code daily — decides B11's ROS and the What is selling screen.
